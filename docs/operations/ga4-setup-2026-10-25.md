## Tracking boundaries

The Google script is inserted only after the visitor accepts the current analytics disclosure. The original first-party-only analytics choice is not sufficient: the added consent version forces a fresh choice before the new vendor runs. Missing or inaccessible consent storage fails closed.

The integration sends explicit public page views (including Next.js navigation), CTA identifiers, deduplicated product interactions and scroll thresholds. Query strings/fragments are removed from page locations; initial external referrers are reduced to their origin. Form values, email addresses, authentication/API routes and deep citation/programme utility routes are not sent by the custom implementation. One page view per actual route transition avoids duplicate initial or React effect views.

Default advertising consent is denied; analytics opt-in updates only analytics_storage, preserving separately managed advertising decisions. Google signals and analytics ad-personalization features are disabled in the GA4 configuration. Analytics opt-in is not advertising consent.

GA cookies are configured to expire after 90 days without expiry renewal. Withdrawal sets GA's disable flag, removes the loader, clears this site's GA cookies at host/root scopes and reloads the page to unload executed vendor code. Navigating into a private/unsupported route similarly unloads an already-running tag. Previously transmitted data is not retroactively erased by consent withdrawal.

Privacy/banner and cookie disclosures were updated to describe the actual optional provider, exclusion rules and withdrawal. Legal pages retain their existing draft-for-review status; this implementation is not a legal compliance certification.
