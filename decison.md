# Nexis Research Institute Decision Record

## Decision

Nexis Research Institute (NRI) will be established as the independent research and innovation division of NexisHub.

NRI will have its own research identity, publication standards, review process, and long-term programme roadmap. It may share infrastructure with NexisHub, but its research conclusions must remain independent of product marketing and commercial goals.

The founding charter supplied for NRI is adopted as the governing research directive for future research design, writing, review, publication, and implementation.

## Purpose

NRI exists to discover, test, explain, and publish useful knowledge in artificial intelligence, software engineering, digital infrastructure, education, healthcare informatics, product engineering, human-computer interaction, cybersecurity, technology policy, and related fields.

Research exists to discover knowledge. Products exist to apply knowledge. Marketing exists to communicate products. These functions must not be confused.

## Mission

NRI will develop research that can be read, studied, criticised, reproduced, debated, cited, and improved by engineers, academics, policymakers, founders, educators, and industry professionals.

The intended practical outcome is better technology and better decisions, not attention or product promotion.

## Strategic ambition

NRI will work toward becoming an Africa-leading industrial AI research institute with global relevance in software engineering, artificial intelligence, education, healthcare, digital transformation, and product innovation.

This is a long-term ambition, not a present-tense claim. Public materials must describe completed work and available evidence accurately.

## Independence from NexisHub products

NRI may research subjects related to SiteNexis, TeachNexis, EventNexis, CareBridge, LogicLand, and future NexisHub products. Product relevance may help select a research question, but it must not determine the result.

NRI publications must not force findings that favour NexisHub. Conflicts, internal data, product involvement, funding, and limitations must be disclosed when relevant.

## Research quality standard

Every publication should pursue:

- novelty
- evidence
- reproducibility
- practical value
- long-term relevance
- scientific integrity
- transparency
- honest uncertainty

When evidence is insufficient, the publication must say so. Conclusions must distinguish observation, interpretation, assumption, and recommendation.

## Editorial standard

NRI writing will be clear, precise, measured, active, and technically responsible. It will avoid marketing language, clickbait, vague claims, buzzwords, inflated certainty, generic AI phrasing, ornamental punctuation, and unnecessary metaphors.

Technical terms should be defined once and used consistently. Short sentences and controlled language should be used where practical. Every paragraph must contribute information or reasoning.

## Publication standard

The default publication structure is:

1. Title and subtitle
2. Authors, version, and publication date
3. Abstract and executive summary
4. Keywords, research questions, and objectives
5. Background and problem statement
6. Current knowledge and literature review where applicable
7. Methodology, data sources, and experimental design
8. Analysis and findings
9. Interpretation, counterarguments, limitations, and threats to validity
10. Practical implications and recommendations
11. Future research and conclusion
12. What would change our mind?
13. Acknowledgements, references, appendices, and glossary

The structure may be shortened for a Research Note, but omitted sections should be omitted deliberately rather than hidden.

## Publication categories

NRI may publish Research Notes, Technical Reports, White Papers, Engineering Papers, Case Studies, Annual Industry Reports, Benchmarks, Framework Papers, Position Papers, Experimental Reports, Literature Reviews, State of Industry Reports, Research Books, and Standards Proposals.

Length must follow the research question. Words must not be added only to reach a target.

## Research ethics and reproducibility

NRI must not manipulate findings, hide inconvenient results, or present estimates as measurements. Publications should disclose sampling, selection criteria, metrics, algorithms, software and model versions, experimental settings, statistical methods, and reproduction instructions whenever applicable.

Figures and tables must teach something. Each figure needs a number, title, caption, interpretation, and source. Each table needs a number, title, and explanation.

## Peer review

Publications should be written for review by software architects, researchers, engineers, designers, students, government agencies, investors, and technical journalists.

Before publication, the review level must be recorded. Possible levels are editorial review, technical review, subject-matter review, independent review, and formal peer review.

## Research divisions

The initial research map includes Artificial Intelligence, AI Safety, AI Agents, AI Evaluation, Machine Learning, LLM Engineering, Knowledge Systems, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Information Retrieval, AI Visibility, Citation Intelligence, Product Engineering, Software Architecture, Distributed Systems, Cloud Computing, Cybersecurity, Human-Computer Interaction, Accessibility, Education Technology, Learning Science, Healthcare Informatics, Data Science, Ethics of AI, Technology Policy, and Innovation Economics.

The map is a planning taxonomy. A division should not claim active research until it has an owner, a defined question, a work plan, and evidence of activity.

## Foundational programmes

The initial NRI roadmap adopts the 30 flagship programmes in the founding charter, including the AI Visibility Index, Citation Intelligence Framework, Machine Trust Score, Future of AI Search, AI Agent Evaluation Frameworks, Responsible AI in African Education, Digital Health Readiness Index, Sustainable SaaS Architecture, Enterprise Knowledge Graph Architecture, African AI Readiness Index, AI and Digital Sovereignty, and Autonomous Enterprises.

Each programme must define its motivation, research questions, expected outcomes, methodology, datasets, possible collaborators, potential impact, and future products enabled.

## Long-term outputs

NRI may develop annual reports, industry indices, engineering frameworks, design frameworks, technical standards, benchmark datasets, evaluation tools, open-source libraries, reference implementations, engineering playbooks, educational material, and policy recommendations.

An output should be published only when its evidence, ownership, maintenance plan, and intended audience are clear.

## Institute identity and information architecture

NRI will be presented as a distinct institute within the NexisHub ecosystem. The planned information architecture is:

```text
NexisHub
└── Nexis Research Institute
    ├── Publications
    ├── White Papers
    ├── Technical Reports
    ├── Annual Indices
    ├── Research Programmes
    ├── Labs
    ├── Benchmarks
    ├── Open Data
    ├── Open Source
    ├── Fellows
    ├── Collaborations
    └── Research Ethics
```

The visual identity may be related to NexisHub, but the institute should feel more deliberate, documentary, and research-oriented than a product marketing surface.

## Decision boundaries

The following remain open decisions and must not be invented in public materials:

- named researchers, fellows, advisors, or collaborators
- institutional partnerships
- funding, grants, or financial projections
- claims of academic accreditation or formal peer review
- datasets that have not been cleared for publication
- legal entity, jurisdiction, and research governance details
- launch date and staffing model

## Success measure

NRI success will not be measured primarily by page views. The relevant measures are whether engineers build better systems because of its work, researchers cite it, educators teach from it, organisations improve because of its recommendations, and future readers still find the work useful.

## Status

Accepted as the working founding decision for NRI. Requires founder review before public institute launch and before any publication claims formal institutional status.
