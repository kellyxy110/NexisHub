# NRI Programme Brief: AI Visibility Index

## Status

Proposed first flagship programme for Nexis Research Institute.

This document is a research plan. It is not a published index, validated score, or claim about the performance of any organisation.

## Motivation

Search and AI-assisted discovery systems expose different parts of the web content pipeline. Organisations can have technically available pages that are difficult to interpret, retrieve, represent, or cite. Existing measures often combine technical search signals, traffic, rankings, and generated-answer observations without clearly separating what is observed from what is inferred.

The AI Visibility Index will investigate whether a transparent, multi-dimensional framework can describe machine discovery conditions without presenting a modelled score as a guaranteed ranking or citation probability.

## Primary research question

Can a transparent set of technical, semantic, retrieval, evidence, and representation measures describe meaningful differences in how organisations are discovered and represented by AI-assisted information systems?

## Secondary questions

1. Which measurable website conditions are most consistently associated with successful source retrieval?
2. Which conditions are associated with accurate organisation and product representation?
3. How stable are AI-assisted discovery observations across systems, prompts, dates, locations, and modes?
4. Which measures are directly observable, and which are only diagnostic proxies?
5. Can independent reviewers reproduce the classification of access, clarity, evidence, and representation failures?
6. Which changes improve the source itself without creating misleading optimisation incentives?

## Proposed dimensions

The initial framework should investigate, without assuming final validity:

- access and crawlability
- canonical identity
- document and information architecture
- entity clarity
- semantic section quality
- internal relationship structure
- structured data accuracy
- evidence and provenance
- freshness and maintenance
- observed retrieval presence
- representation accuracy
- downstream referral or discovery outcomes

These dimensions must remain separate until analysis demonstrates that aggregation is justified.

## Definitions

### AI visibility

The observed ability of a source to be discovered, understood, retrieved, represented, and attributed in an AI-assisted information task.

### Retrieval presence

An observation that content from a source was selected or used in a declared test context.

### Citation presence

An observation that an interface attributed or linked to a source. Citation presence does not establish accuracy or business value.

### Representation accuracy

The degree to which a generated or summarised description matches the source's documented facts, scope, and limitations.

### Diagnostic proxy

A measurable signal used to investigate a possible condition. A proxy is not proof of causal influence.

## Methodology

### Stage 1: framework specification

Define the dimensions, units of analysis, scoring rules, data requirements, and known limitations before collecting outcome data.

### Stage 2: sample design

Create a declared sample of websites or organisations using transparent inclusion criteria. Record sector, size, language, country, technical platform, publication volume, and other variables that may affect interpretation.

Avoid presenting a convenience sample as representative of all organisations.

### Stage 3: technical and content audit

Collect reproducible observations about response status, canonical URLs, robots controls, rendered content, navigation, headings, entity definitions, structured data, sources, authorship, dates, and internal links.

The collection tool, version, crawl date, request limits, and exclusions must be recorded.

### Stage 4: declared discovery tests

Create a fixed test set of questions by intent. Record the exact prompt or query, system, mode, date, location where relevant, result, selected source, citation behaviour, and representation accuracy.

Exploratory tests must be kept separate from the fixed test set.

### Stage 5: independent review

Use at least two reviewers for a sample of classifications. Record disagreements and revise definitions before presenting agreement results.

Reviewers should not know the expected score when that knowledge could influence classification.

### Stage 6: analysis

Report distributions, missing data, uncertainty, reviewer agreement, subgroup differences, and sensitivity to scoring choices. Use correlation carefully. Do not claim that a measurable signal caused a retrieval or citation outcome without a design that supports causal inference.

### Stage 7: replication

Publish enough method, sample, code, version information, and de-identified data for an independent researcher to reproduce the analysis where legal, ethical, and contractual conditions permit.

## Data requirements

- public page URLs
- crawl and response observations
- rendered document extracts
- canonical and robots observations
- structured data snapshots
- internal-link graph data
- entity and claim annotations
- declared discovery test outputs
- reviewer labels
- optional referral or conversion outcomes with permission

No private customer data should enter the programme without a documented legal basis, consent or contract where required, minimisation, access controls, retention limits, and an approved research protocol.

## Initial outputs

1. Framework paper defining the index and its limitations.
2. Public annotation guide.
3. Reproducible audit protocol.
4. Small benchmark dataset with documented sampling.
5. Technical report on cross-system observation stability.
6. Revision log showing what changed after criticism or replication.

## What the index must not claim

- It must not guarantee ranking, retrieval, citation, recommendation, or conversion.
- It must not imply that one vendor's output represents every AI system.
- It must not treat a proprietary composite score as an objective fact.
- It must not publish customer identities or data without permission.
- It must not present correlation as causation.
- It must not use research language to disguise product marketing.

## Threats to validity

- AI systems change over time.
- Providers expose different modes and evidence.
- Prompts and locations influence observations.
- Website sectors and sizes are not evenly distributed.
- Public pages do not reveal every retrieval process.
- Human reviewers may interpret clarity and accuracy differently.
- A score may encourage gaming if its construction is oversimplified.

## What would change our mind?

We would revise or abandon the index if independent reviewers cannot apply the definitions consistently, if repeated tests show that the observations are too unstable for the intended use, if the dimensions do not distinguish meaningful conditions, or if the framework creates stronger incentives for signal manipulation than for useful content.

## First decision required

Approve the research question, define the initial sample, appoint a research lead, and create the annotation guide before building a public score or index dashboard.
